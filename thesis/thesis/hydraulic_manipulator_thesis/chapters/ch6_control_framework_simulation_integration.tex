\chapter{Control Framework and Simulation Integration}
\label{chap:control}

Chapter~\ref{chap:models} left three actuator representations, each with its own
contract and offline evidence. This chapter puts a controller in front of them
and turns them into three experiments.

Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:dls} describes the classical controller, because it is
where the competence comes from even though it never runs at deployment.
Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:ppo} specifies the policy: what it sees, what it emits,
what stops it from emitting something dangerous, and what it is paid for.
Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:training} explains how the first becomes the second, and
reports a result about that transfer which changed the training protocol of this
thesis. The individual stacks appear only after those three are fixed.

\section{The DLS Teacher and Its Two Roles}
\label{sec:ctrl:dls}

\subsection{Scope and Role of the Controller}

No damped-least-squares layer runs underneath the policy at runtime and
nothing is added to the policy's output; if the policy emits zero, the arm does
not move. The DLS controller is nevertheless the reason the policies work at
all, and it appears twice, both times at training or collection time and never
at deployment (Figure~\ref{fig:ctrl:teacher-student}). It is the
\emph{privileged teacher} whose behaviour the policies are distilled from
(Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:training}), and it is the closed-loop controller that
drove the identification runs of Section~\ref{sec:data:isaaclab}. Calling it a
nominal layer would misdescribe the deployed system and would make every
reported result a property of a controller pair rather than of the policy.

  \caption{Training supervision and deployment. Simulator-only terrain and
  contact signals drive the DLS teacher, which supplies DAgger labels to the

  nominal command is added to the policy output.}
  \label{fig:ctrl:teacher-student}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Privileged Information}

The teacher is \emph{privileged} rather than better tuned: it reads two
quantities that no deployable controller on this machine could obtain, namely
the exact terrain height directly under the cutter, queried from the simulator
rather than inferred from a forward-looking scan, and the true cutter--ground
contact force. Together these turn the hardest part of the task, knowing where
the ground is underneath a $\unit[1.2]{m}$ flail head that is occluding it,
into a lookup.

The teacher uses that privilege in a specific way. Rather than commanding a
height, it runs a contact regulator: it maintains a vertical offset relative to
the ground and adjusts that offset to hold the measured contact force near
$\unit[250]{N}$ within a $\unit[100]{N}$ deadband, pressing down below the band,
backing off proportionally above it, searching downward when contact is lost
and retreating immediately on a force spike. The accumulated offset is clamped
to $[-0.30, -0.05]\,\mathrm{m}$ so the regulator can neither drive the head into
the ground nor lift it clear.

The teacher's advantage is therefore not kinematic. Its inverse is the same DLS
solution available to anything; what it has is contact sensing, and that is the
quantity the student must infer from a noisy forward scan and its own contact
history.

\subsection{From Task Error to Cylinder Command}

The rest of the teacher is conventional and is stated for completeness. A goal
point is maintained in the truck frame: laterally it tracks the verge centre
line, vertically it follows the measured ground height plus the contact
regulator's offset. The task error is the difference between that goal and the
measured cutter position, with the lateral component weighted by a factor of
three, because on this machine lateral error is what the acceptance gate
measures and vertical error is largely handled by contact.

That error is mapped to cylinder space through the damped least-squares inverse
of the measured cutter Jacobian $\bm{J}_c$ of
Section~\ref{sec:sys:task-jacobian},
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:teacher-dls}
  \begin{aligned}
    \bm{J}_c^{\#}(\lambda)
      &= \bm{J}_c^{\mathsf T}
         \left(\bm{J}_c\bm{J}_c^{\mathsf T}
               + \lambda^2\bm{I}\right)^{-1}, \\
    \Delta\bm{x}^{*}_{1:3}
      &= \operatorname{clip}\Bigl(
          g\, \bm{J}_c^{\#}(\lambda)\, \bm{W}\bm{e},\;
          \pm\, \Delta x_{\max}\Bigr),
      \quad g = 0.30,
      \quad \Delta x_{\max} = \unit[3]{mm}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
%
with $\bm{W}$ the lateral weighting and the per-step clamp bounding how far any
cylinder may be asked to move in one tick. Only cylinders~1--3 are solved.
Cylinder~4 is held at the reference extension that keeps the head down, for the
reason given in Section~\ref{sec:sys:platform}. The gain $g$ is small on
purpose: this is a stiff, heavy mechanism in contact, and a resolved-rate
controller that closes quickly on the task error simply excites it.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/ch6/ch6_dls_controller}
  \caption{Feedback realization of the damped least-squares teacher of
  \eqref{eq:ctrl:teacher-dls}. The weighted task error is scaled by $g$, mapped
  to the first three cylinder coordinates by the measured damped inverse
  $\bm{J}_c^{\#}(\lambda)$, and clipped to $\pm\Delta x_{\max}$ per tick. Cylinder~4 is
  held at its head-down reference. Cutter position closes the task-space loop
  and the measured cylinder state updates $\bm{J}_c$ at every step. The loop is

\end{figure}


\subsection{Execution Through the Student Interface}

One implementation detail determines whether distillation converges to something
usable. The teacher's output $\Delta\bm{x}^{*}$ is not written to PhysX
directly: it is converted into the same normalised four-value action the student
emits and pushed through the student's entire action chain into the same plant,
so every label the student imitates is by construction an action it can produce.
A teacher writing targets directly to the plant would generate labels outside
the student's reachable set, giving the imitation error a floor no training
could remove.

\section{Policy Formulation}
\label{sec:ctrl:ppo}

\subsection{Policy Definition}

The deployed controller is a memoryless Gaussian multilayer perceptron. It maps
a $66$-value observation to four normalised actions, one desired motion command
per hydraulic cylinder, and that is the entire control law. Actor and critic
are separate networks with hidden widths $256$--$128$--$64$ and ELU
activations, over empirically normalised observations. This description is of
the base policy, and it is shared by all three stacks: the actor width is the
same $66$ in each, and \ppoAAMFD{} deploys this network frozen and unmodified
rather than a widened variant of it. Two things differ for \ppoAAMFD{} and are
treated where they arise. Its four actions are interpreted as valve commands
rather than as desired cylinder increments
(Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:af-fd-sufficient}), and the deployed controller is this
policy plus an additive residual whose own encoder reads a history window
carried alongside the observation, leaving the base actor's input unchanged
(Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:af-residual}).

The base policy is not itself a residual and has no nominal command to correct.
The \ppoAAMFD{} residual is added around this recoverable frozen base. Temporal
inference is assigned to a separate encoder
(Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:af-encoder}), while the truck's forward motion remains
scripted.

\subsection{The Observation Vector}

Figure~\ref{fig:ctrl:observation} lays out the $66$ channels by group. The
grouping tells the story of what this controller has to reason about: nine
terrain-height samples ahead of the cutter, three task-error channels, sixteen
channels of cylinder state and remaining travel, sixteen describing the
actuator interface, ten of pose and context, and twelve of model error.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/ch6/ch6_observation}
  \caption{The $66$-value policy observation, drawn to scale by group. The
  channels combine measurements, derived quantities and actuator-model
  estimates. No trajectory-family
  label, physical factor vector or latent estimate enters the actor. AF keeps
  this $66$-channel input unchanged and carries its causal history separately.}
  \label{fig:ctrl:observation}
\end{figure}

The last group of twelve channels describes the actuator rather than the task,
carrying its standing error as the cylinder tracking residual, the actuator
prediction residual and the normalised cylinder effort. They exist as a direct
consequence of the policy being memoryless. Forming an error requires
differencing position across two steps and comparing against a prediction issued
on the previous step, and a feedforward network can construct neither from a
single observation, so without these channels the model error the policy is
asked to compensate is absent from the input rather than merely hard to see.
This was measured: across ten training blocks the p01 head alignment sat at
$0.79$--$0.80$ and did not respond to changes in the feedforward source or the
levelling gain.

This group is also what makes the matched pair comparable. Under the ROM the
tracking residual is near zero, because the ROM reaches what it is told, while
under a learned ID--FD path it is the model's standing error. The same
observation contract therefore gives each policy an honest view of its own
actuator's shortcomings, which is what Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:matching}
requires.

\subsection{The Action and Its Bounds}

The policy emits $\bm{a} \in \mathbb{R}^4$, sampled from its Gaussian at
training time and taken at the mean for evaluation. Between that sample and the
PhysX cylinder target sit five bounding stages. Figure~\ref{fig:ctrl:action-chain}
places them in the complete PPO actor--critic loop. The critic is used only
during training; the actor and bounded action interface form the deployed
controller. Each bounding stage can only \emph{reduce} authority and cannot
introduce motion the policy did not request.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/ch6/ch6_action_chain}
  \caption{PPO actor--critic formulation and deployed control path. The actor
  maps the observation to a four-dimensional Gaussian action. Rate limiting,
  exponential filtering, the travel shield, command scaling and the target-lead
  cap bound that action before the selected R, IF or AF actuator path drives
  the PhysX arm. The critic and reward update are training-only; measured state
  closes the deployed loop.}
  \label{fig:ctrl:action-chain}
\end{figure}

The rate limit bounds $|\Delta \bm{a}|$ at $0.25$ per control step and is held
\emph{constant} across every curriculum stage rather than used as a curriculum
knob, by the reachability argument above: the teacher's own command stream has a
structural per-step bound near $0.20$, so a student limit below that makes part
of the label set unreachable. The exponential filter ($\beta = 0.70$ at the
final stage) smooths the command. The limit shield scales the command down, and
only in the direction that consumes headroom, over the last $\unit[20]{mm}$ of
cylinder travel, replacing the teacher's hard clamp into the learned range. The
command scale converts the filtered action into $\unit[4]{mm}$ of cylinder
travel per unit action, and the target integrator accumulates the increments in
reference space, permitted to lead the measured extension by at most
$\unit[18]{mm}$.

This chain describes the cylinder-increment interpretation of the action, which
\ppoROM{} and \ppoIDFD{} use. Under \ppoAAMFD{} the same bounded action is read
as a valve command and scaled to span exactly the admissible valve range, so no
further clamp binds at nominal. The bounding stages that act in cylinder space
therefore do not have the same meaning on that stack, which is one reason its
results are read against its own frozen base
(Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:matching}).

That last cap is an anti-windup device and it is load-bearing. Relaxing it from
$\unit[18]{mm}$ to $\unit[50]{mm}$ halved the lateral error on the two families
demanding a sustained standing offset (\texttt{lane\_change\_hold} from $0.4833$
to $0.2310$, \texttt{boom\_sweep} from $0.3745$ to $0.1602$), showing that the
clamp was bounding the standing effort those paths need, while the same
relaxation on cylinder~4 drove head alignment from $0.9507$ to $0.4636$. The
final task therefore resolves the trade per axis, and this is the configuration
every reported \ppoROM{} result was produced under: $\unit[50]{mm}$ on
cylinders~1 to~3 and $\unit[20]{mm}$ on cylinder~4.

The chain replaces the residual band, since exploration is bounded by how much
motion a single action can command rather than by proximity to a baseline, and
it fixes the plant's capability ceiling: four millimetres of cylinder travel per
step is the entire authority budget, which produces the $\unit[0.240]{m/s}$
lateral rate limit of Section~\ref{sec:sys:capability} from which the reference
paths are derived.

\subsection{The Reward Function}

The reward of the final \ppoROM{} task has ten bounded terms, listed in
Table~\ref{tab:ctrl:reward}. Three are positive and describe the task. Seven
are penalties and describe how the machine must behave while doing it. All are
bounded, and all retain a live gradient beyond the acceptance boundary, so that
a policy already inside a gate still has a direction to improve in.

It replaces an inherited formulation carrying $53$ weighted reward fields, at
which size the optimum is not something a designer can reason about: three
separate terms turned out to be working against the task without being noticed
for thirteen training campaigns. Three rules govern the replacement, each
adopted after a measured failure, and Appendix~\ref{app:ppo-config} records the
measurements behind them.

\paragraph{No Gaussian Shaping}
The earlier reward priced tracking as $w\exp[-(e/s)^2]$, which is effectively
flat beyond about $2s$ and therefore supplies almost no gradient where the
failing path families live. Every shaping term is instead linear or Huber in the
error. Writing $x = |e|/s$, the two shapes used are
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:shapes}
  B(e;s,c) = \min(x,\,c),
  \qquad
  H(e;s,c) = \min\Bigl(c,\;
  \begin{cases}
    \tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}, & x \le 1\\
    x - \tfrac{1}{2},  & x > 1
  \end{cases}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
%
$B$ has the same gradient at $\unit[0.5]{m}$ of error as at $\unit[0.05]{m}$,
so a policy far from the reference is still told which way to move; $H$ buys
quadratic precision near zero where cut height needs it, without a vanishing
far field.

\paragraph{Bounded Terms}
A term that can grow without limit can dominate the sum and destabilize the
critic. Each contribution is clipped, which gives the reward a published budget:
$6+12+4 = 22$ for the task terms against $-24$ for the safety terms. A
non-finite value is replaced by $-25$, just below that worst case, so a
numerical fault can never outscore legitimate behaviour.

\paragraph{Fixed Sign Convention}
The three task terms are non-negative and the seven safety terms non-positive.
This is what makes worst-case (CVaR) weighting safe to add later: scaling a
non-positive term by a factor $\ge 1$ can only lower the return, whereas
up-weighting a \emph{positive} reward for the worst environments pays the policy
to be bad, as measured in the earlier stack.

Three terms of the earlier formulation were removed on measured evidence rather
than preference, and Appendix~\ref{app:ppo-config} records the figures. The
\emph{action-magnitude} penalty on $\operatorname{mean}(a^2)$ charged for
precisely the sustained offset that \texttt{lane\_change\_hold},
\texttt{offset\_return} and \texttt{boom\_sweep} structurally require, so the
policy learned to return to neutral, which is the one thing ``hold an offset''
forbids. The \emph{head barrier} was replaced because the head group dwarfed
lateral tracking, and the \emph{clearance barrier} was disabled because measured
contact, cylinder headroom and cut-height error proved more reliable signals of
the same condition.

One apparent inconsistency is deliberate. The tool-acceleration term saturates
at $\unit[300]{m/s^2}$ while the corresponding gate sits at
$\unit[120]{m/s^2}$. The reward scale is a saturation scale for a bounded
penalty rather than a target, so the gate remains the binding criterion and the
reward's role is only to keep a gradient against gross excursions. The
action-rate term is held mild for a related reason: cylinder~4 owns head pitch,
so a rate tax falls hardest on the fast corrections that keep the head down as
boom attitude shifts.

Every term is logged individually and by name during training, because the whole
difficulty is that total reward can rise while the system gets worse. The reward
is therefore \emph{not} the result criterion: promotion uses the eight physical
gates of Section~\ref{sec:sys:evaluation-criteria}.

\subsection{Optimisation}
\label{sec:ctrl:optimization}

Policies are optimised with clipped-surrogate PPO over generalized advantage
estimation, in the form of Section~\ref{sec:fund:rl-residual}. The maintained
configuration uses clip parameter $\epsilon = 0.2$, discount $\gamma = 0.99$,
GAE parameter $\lambda = 0.95$, five learning epochs over four minibatches per
update, an adaptive learning-rate schedule targeting a KL divergence of $0.02$
from a base rate of $3\times10^{-4}$, gradient-norm clipping at $1.0$, and a
clipped value loss with coefficient $1.0$. The terrain task collects $32$ steps
per environment per update. At $512$ environments that is $16\,384$ transitions
per update, chosen because the inherited $128$-step horizon exceeded two
minutes per update on the available GPU without adding useful batch diversity.

The final fine-tuning blocks depart from that configuration because they run
under the reference anchor of Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:anchored} rather than an
adaptive learning-rate schedule: clip $\epsilon = 0.1$, discount
$\gamma = 0.97$, three learning epochs, fixed learning rates of $10^{-5}$ for
the actor and $10^{-4}$ for the critic, entropy coefficient $0.001$, an
exploration standard deviation fixed at $0.15$ and excluded from the gradient,
and frozen observation normalisers.

Three of those departures are structural rather than tuning. The discount is
reduced because the episode is long in simulation steps but locally controlled,
so an action should be credited for correcting the terrain state it faces rather
than for a return hundreds of steps later that the critic cannot resolve. The
actor learning rate sits an order of magnitude below the critic's so that the
actor is deliberately the slow variable. And the adaptive schedule is dropped
because the anchored formulation already constrains the update through an
explicit divergence bound; two competing step-size controllers would make
neither attributable.

The exploration settings are also specific to this architecture. A zero action
here means the arm does not move at all rather than that it tracks nominally, so
the initial standard deviation was raised from $0.05$ to $0.30$ to let the
policy discover the command directions before refining them. On the terrain
task, where distillation supplies a competent starting point, it is reduced
again and \emph{held fixed} rather than initialized, for the reason taken up in
Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:anchored}. Exact values for any reported run are read from
that run's frozen manifest (Table~\ref{tab:app:ppo}).

\section{Training Protocol and the Distillation Finding}
\label{sec:ctrl:training}

\subsection{Distillation by DAgger}

Policies are initialized by imitation, not by reward. The privileged teacher is
rolled out in the student's own environment and the student is fitted to the
teacher's raw action by regression, in the DAgger scheme of Ross et
al.~\cite{ross2011reduction}: the teacher's probability of \emph{executing} the
action, rather than merely labelling it, is annealed from one to zero over the
first $70\,\%$ of iterations, so later training collects states the student
itself visits. Labels are accumulated in a replay buffer of $262\,144$
transitions and refitted over three epochs per iteration in minibatches of
$8\,192$.

Figure~\ref{fig:ctrl:distillation} shows a complete distillation run for the
ROM stack, taken from the stored record. The behaviour-cloning loss falls by
more than an order of magnitude while the teacher is still driving, reaches its
minimum around the handover, and then \emph{rises} once the student is alone.
That rise is the distribution shift DAgger exists to expose rather than a
failure: the student is now visiting states the teacher never chose, and the
teacher's advice on those states is new information. The closed-loop task
metrics remain near the teacher's preflight values without matching them.

\subsection{Objective Mismatch in Unconstrained Fine-Tuning}
\label{sec:ctrl:finding}

Unconstrained PPO fine-tuning from distilled checkpoints increased total reward
while degrading the primary tracking objective, in the worst case by a factor
of $12.9$. The critic remained healthy, with explained variance in the
$0.85$--$0.95$ range. This motivates the anchored formulation of
Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:anchored}, which is used for the final policies.

The reward includes ground contact and cut height as well as lateral tracking.
It can therefore increase while a gate-relevant tracking measure degrades.

This is a claim about two functionals over the same policy space rather
than about the optimiser. Training maximizes the expected return
$J(\vartheta)$, while acceptance is decided by the vector of physical gates
$\bm{G}(\vartheta) \preceq \bm{g}^{\star}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:sys:evaluation-criteria}. Writing
$\mathcal{F} = \{\vartheta : \bm{G}(\vartheta) \preceq \bm{g}^{\star}\}$ for the
feasible set, the measured content of the finding is
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:mismatch}
  \arg\max_{\vartheta} J(\vartheta) \notin \mathcal{F},
  \qquad\text{while}\qquad
  \vartheta_{\mathrm{distilled}} \in \mathcal{F}.
\end{equation}
%
Distillation lands inside $\mathcal{F}$ because the teacher was constructed to
satisfy the gates, not to maximize $J$. Unconstrained ascent on $J$ from that
point therefore leaves $\mathcal{F}$ by construction. This is the optimiser
working correctly on the wrong functional, and it is why the remedy is a
constraint on the ascent rather than a repair of the reward.

Two consequences follow, both of them protocol. Unconstrained fine-tuning is
inadmissible, and both the pre- and post-fine-tune checkpoints are reported in
every case. And reward decomposition, critic explained variance, policy entropy
and the gate metrics themselves are logged \emph{during} training rather than
only at the end, since a training curve reporting only the scalar reward would
have shown a success here.

The general form applies beyond this machine: when a competent teacher exists
and the acceptance criterion is not the reward, reward-based fine-tuning is not
automatically an improvement step. Distillation transfers the intent, PPO then
optimises the proxy, and the two point in measurably different directions. That
divergence grows with distance travelled from the policy where proxy and intent
were measured to agree, so the remedy is to bound the distance explicitly rather
than to abandon reward optimisation.

\section{Reference-Anchored PPO}
\label{sec:ctrl:anchored}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ctrl:mismatch} says that reward ascent leaves the feasible
set. It does not say that reward ascent is useless, only that it is unbounded.
The construction of this section keeps PPO and constrains how far it may travel
from a policy already known to satisfy the gates. Writing $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$
for a frozen reference policy and $J$ for the expected return, each training
block solves
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:constrained}
  \max_{\vartheta} J(\vartheta)
  \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
  \mathbb{E}_{\bm{o}\sim d_{\pi_\vartheta}}
  \bigl[ \mathrm{KL}\bigl( \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\,|\,\bm{o})
  \,\|\, \pi_\vartheta(\cdot\,|\,\bm{o}) \bigr) \bigr]
  \le \delta_{\mathrm{ref}},
\end{equation}
%
with the expectation taken over the states the policy itself visits.

\subsection{Critic Warm-Up}

After distillation the actor already behaves sensibly, but the critic has never
been fitted to the long terrain return, so the first PPO updates would move a
useful actor using a value estimate that is not yet meaningful. The actor is
frozen for $150$ iterations while only the value loss is optimised, under the
asserted invariant $\vartheta_{\text{after}} = \vartheta_{\text{before}}$. The
observation normalisers are frozen at the same point, since otherwise the same
raw observation is normalised differently from one iteration to the next and the
actor's behaviour changes while its weights do not.

\subsection{The Reference Anchor}

Let $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be a frozen copy of the accepted warm actor. It is
queried only on states the live policy visits and never supplies an executed
action, so PPO remains the sole controller of the plant. The penalty is the
\emph{forward} divergence, which for diagonal Gaussians is available in closed
form,
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:kl}
  D_{\mathrm{ref}}
  = \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}} \,\|\, \pi_\vartheta\bigr)
  = \sum_i \left[
    \log\frac{\sigma_{\vartheta,i}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{ref},i}}
    + \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{ref},i}^{2}
      + (\mu_{\mathrm{ref},i}-\mu_{\vartheta,i})^{2}}
      {2\sigma_{\vartheta,i}^{2}}
    - \frac{1}{2}\right],
\end{equation}
%
at the cost of one additional forward pass and no sampling. Because both scales
are fixed at $\sigma = 0.15$, this collapses to a pure quadratic on the drift of
the commanded action,
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:kl-fixed}
  D_{\mathrm{ref}}
  = \sum_i \frac{(\mu_{\mathrm{ref},i}-\mu_{\vartheta,i})^{2}}{2(0.15)^{2}},
\end{equation}
%
which is the second reason for fixing $\sigma$: with a free standard deviation,
entropy collapse shrinks $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ while the commanded action moves, so
the constraint would report compliance during the drift it exists to prevent.

The direction is load-bearing. Forward KL is mass-covering: it charges
$\pi_\vartheta$ wherever $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ has density and $\pi_\vartheta$
does not, and therefore penalizes the \emph{abandonment} of reference
behaviour. The reverse direction is mode-seeking and would permit the policy to
collapse onto a subset of reference behaviour at no cost, which is the observed failure, in which one competence was dropped to buy another. It
follows that the term is a behaviour-cloning anchor evaluated on the policy's
own state distribution, rather than the reverse-KL regularizer of soft
reinforcement learning. Its lineage is demonstration-regularized policy
gradient~\cite{rajeswaran2018dapg}, not the Boltzmann-optimum family.

The actor objective is
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:anchored-loss}
  \mathcal{L}(\vartheta)
  = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PPO}}
  + \mathcal{L}_{V}
  - \beta\,\mathcal{H}(\pi_\vartheta)
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\,
    \mathbb{E}_k\bigl[\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}} \| \pi_\vartheta)\bigr],
  \qquad
  \lambda_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.10 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Trust Regions and Rollback}

A penalty is not a constraint: at a fixed coefficient, a sufficiently large
advantage always buys its way past. Feasibility is therefore also verified on the update taken. Two bounds are imposed,
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:bounds}
  D_{\mathrm{update}} = \mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\mathrm{old}} \| \pi_\vartheta)
  \le 0.01,
  \qquad
  D_{\mathrm{ref}} \le 0.05,
\end{equation}
%
and they do different work. $D_{\mathrm{update}}$ is the classical step-size
control that keeps the surrogate a valid local model of $J$.
$D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the new object: because the anchor is \emph{fixed} within
a block rather than moving with the policy, it bounds cumulative drift. A
sequence of updates each individually satisfying $D_{\mathrm{update}}$ can
still walk arbitrarily far from feasibility, and that is the trajectory the earlier campaigns took, monotonically, with the reward rising the whole
way. Only a constraint referred to a fixed feasible point can observe it.

Enforcement is in two stages. During minibatch optimisation the epoch is
early-stopped as soon as $D_{\mathrm{update}}$ exceeds its bound, and after the
update both divergences are recomputed over the full rollout. If either bound
fails, or any quantity is non-finite, the actor and its optimiser state are
restored \emph{atomically}. On a divergence rejection the critic fit is retained
when finite, since the bounds constrain the policy rather than the value
function; on a non-finite update both networks are restored. In the final block,
five of one hundred logged updates were rejected, all on the reference bound and
none on the update bound, so the drift caught was of the kind ordinary PPO
clipping does not see.

\subsection{The Block Ratchet}

The reference is not fixed forever. Within a block it is frozen. When a
checkpoint is scored and passes the gates, it is promoted to reference,
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:ratchet}
  \vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(m+1)} = \vartheta^{\star},
  \qquad
  \vartheta^{\star} \in \mathcal{F},
  \quad
  \vartheta^{\star} \in
  B_{\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}}\bigl(\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(m)}\bigr),
\end{equation}
%
and the actor's optimiser moments are reset, because momentum accumulated
relative to the old anchor is not meaningful relative to the new one. The
critic's are retained: it is fitting the same return, and its calibration is
expensive to rebuild.

This is the structural point of the construction. The policy is at all times
within a bounded divergence of a checkpoint that has been verified against
$\bm{G}$, and the anchor advances only through verified checkpoints. The
acceptance criterion therefore enters the training loop instead of appearing
only at the end. The optimiser still ascends the proxy. It simply can never
travel far from the last point at which proxy and intent were measured to
agree. In this construction distillation is no longer the endpoint: it is
$\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(0)}$.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ctrl:anchored} summarises the procedure in five stages.
The first two produce the initial verified anchor. Stages~3 and~4 repeat for
each PPO block, and Stage~5 returns the last promoted checkpoint.

\begin{algorithm}[!ht]
  \small
  \DontPrintSemicolon
  \SetKwInOut{Input}{input}\SetKwInOut{Output}{output}
  \Input{teacher $\pi_T$; gate test
    $\bm{G}(\vartheta)\preceq\bm{g}^{\star}$; $M$ PPO blocks; anchored loss
    and divergence bounds from Equations~\eqref{eq:ctrl:anchored-loss}
    and~\eqref{eq:ctrl:bounds}}
  \Output{last promoted actor $\vartheta^{\star}$ satisfying the gate test}
  \tcp{\textbf{Stage 1 -- Distil a feasible actor}}
  $\mathcal{D}\leftarrow$ DAgger states labelled by teacher mean action
    $\bm{y}^{T}$\;
  $\vartheta \leftarrow \arg\min_{\vartheta}
    \mathbb{E}_{(\bm{o},\bm{y}^{T})\sim\mathcal{D}}
    \bigl[\|\mu_{\vartheta}(\bm{o})-\bm{y}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr]$\;
  remove $\pi_T$ from the controller
    \tcp*{all later rollouts are teacher-free}
  \tcp{\textbf{Stage 2 -- Warm the critic and set the first anchor}}
  freeze $\vartheta$ and the observation normalisers\;
  \For{$j\leftarrow1$ \KwTo $150$}{
    roll out $\pi_{\vartheta}$ and set
    $\psi \leftarrow \psi - \eta_{\mathrm{critic}}
      \nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{L}_{V}(\psi)$\;
  }
  verify $\vartheta_{\mathrm{after}}=\vartheta_{\mathrm{before}}$\;
  $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}\leftarrow\vartheta$;
    $\vartheta^{\star}\leftarrow\vartheta$
    \tcp*{initial verified anchor}
  \For{block $m\leftarrow0$ \KwTo $M-1$}{
    \tcp{\textbf{Stage 3 -- Train inside the frozen reference bound}}
    freeze $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ for the whole block\;
    \For{each PPO iteration in block $m$}{
      collect an on-policy rollout with $\pi_{\vartheta}$ as sole controller\;
      compute and normalise GAE; save snapshot $\mathcal{S}$;
        set $\vartheta_{\mathrm{old}}\leftarrow\vartheta$\;
      \For{$3$ epochs over the rollout minibatches}{
        \lIf{$\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\mathrm{old}}\|\pi_{\vartheta})
          >\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}$}{stop the update epochs}
        update actor and critic using Equation~\eqref{eq:ctrl:anchored-loss}\;
      }
      compute $(D_{\mathrm{upd}},D_{\mathrm{ref}})$ on the full rollout
        \tcp*{full rollout}
      \uIf{$D_{\mathrm{upd}}$ or $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is non-finite}{
        restore the complete snapshot $\mathcal{S}$\;
      }
      \uElseIf{$D_{\mathrm{upd}}>\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}$ \textbf{or}
        $D_{\mathrm{ref}}>\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}$}{
        restore the actor and its optimiser moments from $\mathcal{S}$;
          retain the new critic fit\;
      }
    }
    \tcp{\textbf{Stage 4 -- Evaluate the block and promote a passing checkpoint}}
    score every saved checkpoint on at least three held-out terrain meshes\;
    aggregate each gate to the worst mesh and, for tracking gates, the worst
      path family\;
    $\mathcal{C}\leftarrow
      \{\vartheta_c:\bm{G}(\vartheta_c)\preceq\bm{g}^{\star}\}$\;
    \eIf{$\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing$}{
      choose $\vartheta^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}$ using the fixed checkpoint
        selection rule\;
      load $\vartheta\leftarrow\vartheta^{\star}$ and set
        $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}\leftarrow\vartheta^{\star}$\;
      reset the actor optimiser moments; retain the critic state\;
    }{
      leave $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ unchanged; do not promote the block\;
    }
  }
  \tcp{\textbf{Stage 5 -- Return the deployable policy}}
  \Return{$\vartheta^{\star}=\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}$}\;
  \caption{Five-stage reference-anchored PPO procedure. The teacher is used
  only in Stage~1. All PPO rollouts are controlled by $\pi_{\vartheta}$ alone,
  and the frozen reference moves only when a checkpoint passes every gate.}
  \label{alg:ctrl:anchored}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Theoretical Status}

The components are standard and citable. The bound on $D_{\mathrm{update}}$ is
conservative policy iteration and its trust-region
descendants~\cite{kakade2002approximately,schulman2015trust}; the atomic
rollback is that literature's backtracking line search reduced to a single
trial point; the penalty in Equation~\eqref{eq:ctrl:anchored-loss} is a
Lagrangian relaxation of the constrained problem~\eqref{eq:ctrl:constrained} at
a fixed multiplier, with the rollback supplying the feasibility enforcement a
fixed multiplier cannot.

What does not follow is a certificate. Bounding the divergence from a verified
policy does bound gate degradation in form, since the chain rule of KL over
trajectories, Pinsker's inequality and the boundedness of every gate metric give
$|\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\vartheta}[g] - \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}[g]|
\le 2 G_{\max}\sqrt{H \delta_{\mathrm{ref}}/2}$ for a metric $g$ with
$\|g\|_\infty \le G_{\max}$ over a horizon $H$. At the production horizon of
$2808$ steps and $\delta_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.05$ the right-hand side exceeds
eight, which for a metric bounded in $[0,1]$ is vacuous, and there is a further
measure mismatch because the constraint is taken under the live policy's state
distribution while the chain rule requires the reference's. The construction is
therefore a principled restriction of reward ascent to a neighbourhood of a
verified policy, not a guarantee: the gates certify each promotion.

\section{R: Reference Actuator Baseline}
\label{sec:ctrl:ppo-rom}

\ppoROM{} is the reference condition of this thesis. The policy emits desired
cylinder motion, the frozen command ROM converts it to a cylinder position
target, and PhysX closes the loop. There is no online adaptation anywhere in
the stack. The truck is scripted over a $\unit[26]{m}$ strip at one constant
speed drawn per episode from $2$ to $\unit[7]{km/h}$, the eleven path families
of Section~\ref{sec:sys:task} are distributed across environments within a
single run, and the terrain mesh is drawn per environment so that no policy can
memorize one ground surface.

Its status is that of a reference condition rather than a competitor. The
command ROM is a Simscape-trained model with a bounded closed-loop calibration
against IsaacLab and PhysX, and ROM-plus-PhysX \emph{is} the deployment plant,
so \ppoROM{}'s actuator coincides with the plant. The consequence for how its
tracking advantage may be read is stated once, in
Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:matching}.

The policy is produced by the three-layer construction of this chapter:
distillation from the privileged DLS teacher to a feasible student, critic
warm-up with the actor frozen, and reference-anchored PPO in blocks under
Equations~\eqref{eq:ctrl:anchored-loss} and~\eqref{eq:ctrl:bounds}. The final
block produced checkpoints $280$ to $329$. The last iterate was not taken,
because PPO can raise mean return while damaging a single path family.
Candidates were scored on held-out meshes against the eight gates, aggregated to
the worst mesh and, for tracking-critical gates, the worst path family.
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} reports the final common-protocol evaluation.

\section{IF: Non-Adaptive Learned Baseline}
\label{sec:ctrl:ppo-idfd}

\ppoIDFD{} subclasses the \ppoROM{} task and changes exactly one thing. The
policy still emits desired cylinder motion, but that command is now realised by
the frozen inverse model of Section~\ref{sec:models:id}, which converts it to a
valve command, followed by the frozen strict forward model of
Section~\ref{sec:models:fd}, which converts the valve command to a predicted
extension increment. PhysX receives the same bounded cylinder target as before.
Task, terrain families, meshes, observation contract, action chain, reward,
gates and the PPO update are inherited unchanged, so the actuator
representation is the only free variable between the two stacks.

The stack is the honest control for \ppoROM, whose actuator coincides with the
deployment plant, and it is the stack in which the inverse model is carried in
the command path and is adequate there.

\emph{Robust} is used here in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:fund:domain-randomization}: the stack was trained across the
randomised plant family and applies one fixed response to whichever member of
that family it meets, performing no online estimation and conditioning on no
plant identity. It does not mean the policy is blind to plant variation, since
the twelve model-error channels carry a standing residual that is itself
plant-dependent. What the stack lacks is not information about the consequences
of plant variation but any representation of \emph{which} plant produced them.

Two properties of the composed path shape its behaviour, and both were
established offline. The valve command is accumulated rather than issued
absolutely and is clipped to the per-axis limits $[0.95, 0.95, 0.80, 0.80]$, so
the actuator path carries its own saturation boundary independently of the
action chain. Only the predicted extension increment is integrated; the velocity
output is retained as a diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:models:fd}).

The stored run manifest shows that the actor learning rate, entropy coefficient,
seed, terrain seed, environment count, curriculum stage, frozen normaliser, and
warm-up match \ppoROM{} field for field (Table~\ref{tab:app:ppo}). This makes
the pair a controlled comparison. Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} reports the
common-protocol results.

used as baselines. Both use the same policy interface and PhysX task; only the

\section{AF: The Adaptive Stack}
\label{sec:ctrl:ppo-aamfd}

\ppoAAMFD{} places online adaptation on the forward model alone. The policy
emits the valve command directly, the frozen strict forward model of
Section~\ref{sec:models:fd} converts that command to a predicted extension
increment, and PhysX receives the same bounded cylinder target as the other two
stacks:
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:af-path}
  \pi_{\mathrm{frozen}} + \text{history residual}
  \;\longrightarrow\; \bm{u}
  \;\longrightarrow\; f_{\mathrm{FD}}
  \;\longrightarrow\; \text{PhysX} .
\end{equation}
%
The inverse model is absent from the command path. This is the one structural
difference from \ppoIDFD{}, and it is deliberate: the name of the stack records
the two models that remain, \AAM{} and \FD{}. Task, terrain families, meshes,
observation contract, reward, gates and the PPO update are inherited unchanged,
so the actuator representation and the adaptation path are the only free
variables against the baselines.

That the inverse model is genuinely out of the loop is a verified property
rather than a claim about intent. Under the direct-valve configuration the
adaptation-site instrumentation reports zero valve elements attributable to the
inverse model and zero adapter applications at that site across a complete run,
where all three counters would be non-zero if the inverse model were in the
path (Appendix~\ref{app:models:residual}). The actuator key recorded inside the
checkpoints still reads \texttt{id\_aam\_fd}, retained so that previously
trained checkpoints remain loadable; it is a legacy identifier, not a
description of the runtime path.

\subsection{Omission of the Inverse Model}
\label{sec:ctrl:af-no-id}

The inverse model's place in two of the three stacks follows from what each
stack is for. The command ROM together with PhysX \emph{is} the deployment plant
(Section~\ref{sec:models:rom-calibration}), so \ppoROM{} uses the plant itself
and fixes the reference condition. The inverse and forward models are identified
independently of it, from the high-fidelity Simscape data of
Chapter~\ref{chap:data}, and neither has seen the deployment plant during
training, so \ppoIDFD{} prices the cost of substituting an independently
identified pair for the plant itself. That question requires the inverse model:
a policy commanding desired cylinder motion needs a map from that intent to a
valve command, and supplying that map from identification data alone is exactly
the substitution being priced. The R--IF gap is interpretable only because the
composition is complete.

The adaptive stack asks a different question. The inverse model remains
appropriate for \ppoIDFD{} and its one-step transfer is established in
Section~\ref{sec:models:id-trust}. In the adaptive command path, however, an
additional correction must retain authority after the command is formed.

The same transfer study recorded the failure mode that later disqualified it for
adaptation. The model predicts a valve-command \emph{increment}, and the
deployed command is the accumulated, clipped quantity
$\bm{u}_k = \operatorname{clip}(\bm{u}_{k-1} + \Delta\bm{u}_k, -\bm{u}_{\max},
\bm{u}_{\max})$ with per-axis limits $[0.95,\, 0.95,\, 0.80,\, 0.80]$, so under
sustained demand the accumulation walks the command onto its own clip and holds
it there. Fed its own predictions, the composite left the reference by more than
a metre in \emph{every one} of the $118$ runs, with its pre-clip valve command
at the limit on $89.1\,\%$ of elements while the ROM valve on the same runs
never reached its bound. Closed-loop instrumentation over the $33$-cell panel
measures the same thing in the terms that matter for adaptation: the command sat
at its pre-clip limit on $79.9\,\%$ of valve elements, $34.5\,\%$ of the
corrections the adapter computed were clipped away after being computed, and on
$37.9\,\%$ of elements the correction could not move the command at all.

The quantity being adapted and the quantity being saturated are therefore the
same, and the consequence is not a small effect but an uninterpretable one: a
null measured through such a path cannot distinguish a task that is not
adaptation-limited from credit that was clipped before it could reach the
metric. The disqualification is specific and retracts nothing claimed for
\ppoIDFD{}, where the command is consumed as issued and saturation is a limit on
authority rather than a defect of the map. It binds only where a second signal
must be added to that command and survive the same clip.

The final stack therefore emits the valve command directly. The residual acts
on the same bounded command as the policy, rather than on an increment that is
subsequently accumulated and clipped.

\subsection{Sufficiency of the Forward Model}
\label{sec:ctrl:af-fd-sufficient}

Removing one of the two learned actuator models raises the question of whether
the remaining one carries the path. Sufficiency here is a specific claim: the
forward model must convert a commanded valve position into a one-step extension
increment with genuine valve sensitivity, because that increment is the only
quantity the controller integrates.

The evidence is the strict retraining of
Section~\ref{sec:models:fd-identifiability}. Before it, zeroing the valve input
at a $\unit[0.5]{s}$ horizon changed the rollout-error ratio to only $0.94$,
meaning the network had extrapolated smooth closed-loop kinematics rather than
learned the valve's effect. After strict retraining on the open-loop plateau
data the valve-zero error ratio rises to $1.81$, extension rollout RMSE falls
from $\unit[75.2]{mm}$ to $\unit[12.4]{mm}$ at $\unit[0.5]{s}$ and from
$\unit[157.9]{mm}$ to $\unit[27.3]{mm}$ at $\unit[1.0]{s}$, and per-axis
one-step extension $R^2$ improves from $[-5.7,\, 0.60]$ to $[0.76,\, 0.98]$.

Sufficiency is established on the channel that is used and is not claimed beyond
it. Three qualifications carry forward. Only the extension increment is
integrated, the velocity output remaining weak on two axes and retained as a
diagnostic. Zero-order hold remains competitive below $\unit[0.25]{s}$, so the
forward model earns its place at the horizons the controller actually spans
rather than uniformly. And the valve authority the model represents is strongly
operating-point dependent, varying by a factor of $15$--$75$ across pressure
quartiles and capable of changing sign, which is why a constant valve Jacobian
was rejected.

\subsection{The Residual Controller}
\label{sec:ctrl:af-residual}

Adaptation enters as an additive, bounded residual on the valve command:
%
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ctrl:af-residual}
  \bm{u} = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(
    \bm{u}_0 + \alpha \tanh
    \bigl( g_\psi(\bm{o},\, \phi(\bm{H}_t)) \bigr),\, -1,\, 1 \bigr),
  \qquad
  \bm{u}_0 = \pi_{\mathrm{frozen}}(\bm{o}) ,
\end{equation}
%
where $\phi$ is a temporal-convolution encoder over a window of
$(\text{state},\, \text{action})$ pairs and $g_\psi$ is a small
multilayer perceptron producing a per-cylinder correction. Only $\phi$ and
$g_\psi$ are trained: $53\,836$ parameters against a $59\,588$-parameter frozen
base.

Three construction choices make the comparison in
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} a single-variable one, and each was verified
rather than assumed. The final layer of $g_\psi$ is zero-initialised and
$\tanh(0) = 0$, so at construction the residual is exactly zero and the wrapped
policy reproduces the frozen base bit for bit, at a measured maximum absolute
action difference of $0.0$. Every arm of an ablation therefore begins as the
identical policy, and any end-state difference is attributable to the residual
having been learned rather than to the task having been relearned. The
correction is bounded by $\alpha\tanh(\cdot)$ regardless of what the head
produces: with head weights drawn from $\mathcal{N}(0,\, 50)$ the output
saturates at exactly $\alpha$ and remains finite, so a bad update degrades
accuracy instead of emitting a non-finite action, which matters because a
non-finite action written into the history buffer would poison the entire
window. And the residual scale can be set to zero at evaluation time without
touching any other part of the stack, in which case the stack reduces to the
frozen base exactly.

This last property improves on the earlier latent-conditioned design, whose
actor was itself a $68$-input network, so that disabling the residual restored
the baseline actuator path but not the baseline actor and the ablation was never
quite single-variable. Under \ppoAAMFD{} the base actor is the distilled policy
unmodified, the residual carries the whole of the context dependence, and
disabling it recovers the baseline in full.

The history window is carried in the observation vector rather than in a buffer
held inside the module, because PPO calls the actor many times per update epoch
and a module-held buffer would be advanced by the optimisation loop and
desynchronise from the simulation. The causal convention is fixed throughout:
the estimate at step $t$ uses $\bm{x}_{t-k:t-1}$ and $\bm{a}_{t-k:t-1}$ and does
not include step $t$.

\subsection{Freezing the Base Policy}
\label{sec:ctrl:af-frozen}

Freezing the base is structural rather than a tuning preference, and the
alternative was closed by measurement before the residual route was adopted.
Under a trajectory-regularised fine-tune with a reference-KL anchor the policy
is stable but cannot acquire a new input dependence, because the anchor's
reference is context-blind by construction, so any context-dependent behaviour
increases KL to that reference and is penalised. A gradient multiplier of $1000$
applied to the context columns alone moved action sensitivity only from
$0.001\,\%$ to $0.004\,\%$ of action scale over $400$ iterations, and masking
the anchor at $\bm{z}=\bm{0}$ reached $0.005\,\%$. The anchor is not the
blocker, however: without it PPO walks away from the distilled behaviour,
trading line tracking for ground contact because that raises its objective.

A residual head has no anchor. It is new capacity whose only function is to
depend on the latent, so nothing opposes that dependence. Measured on the same
stack, the trained residual's action responds to its history at $3.35\,\%$ of
action scale, roughly $840$ times the anchored route.

\subsection{Training of the Adaptation Encoder}
\label{sec:ctrl:af-encoder}

The encoder $\phi$ and the head $g_\psi$ are trained together under the
reinforcement objective, not fitted offline to an auxiliary target and handed
to a controller afterwards. This is the concurrent scheme of Ji et
al.~\cite{ji2022concurrent}, and its consequence is that the latent is
control-relevant by construction rather than by hope: the only gradient
reaching $\phi$ is the one that improves the task.

An offline dynamics objective does not by itself guarantee a control-relevant
history representation. The adaptation module is therefore assessed
behaviourally in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}, through action sensitivity to
history and the closed-loop comparison with the frozen base.

\subsection{Consistency With the Plant Dynamics}
\label{sec:ctrl:af-consistency}

Two consistency requirements follow from the system model of
Chapter~\ref{chap:system}, and both are met by construction.

The first is causality. Each history row carries observable quantities only,
namely joint positions, joint velocities, cylinder pressure differences and the
previous valve command. The privileged factor vector, the true latent, the
reference, future rows and any hidden simulator state are excluded, and the
estimate at step $t$ is formed without step $t$, so nothing enters the deployed
path that would be unavailable on the physical machine.

The second is a qualification rather than a guarantee. Under \ppoAAMFD{} the
pressure differences supplied to the encoder are recursive states of the frozen
forward model, not measurements of the plant, so the encoder observes the plant
through the forward model's representation of it and its estimate is consistent
with the true dynamics only to the extent that the forward model is.
Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:af-fd-sufficient} bounds that extent. This limit on the
interpretation of the latent is carried into Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}
rather than resolved here.

The stack does not claim a physically named estimate. The residual is trained to
improve the task, and the quantity $\phi$ extracts is whatever serves that
objective; it is not constrained to correspond to seal condition, compliance,
supply pressure or any other named factor. Recovering named physical quantities
would require an explicit plant model and a different estimator, noted as future
work in Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}.

\subsection{Scope of the Claims}
\label{sec:ctrl:af-claims}

The stack is complete and its interfaces are verified. The residual authority
$\alpha$ is a genuine tuned hyperparameter and is reported as one rather than
presented as a natural constant. The deployed value is $\alpha = 0.5$. At
$\alpha = 0.15$ the residual is not merely less effective but actively worse
than the base it corrects, by $28.3\,\%$ on cut-height error paired across
$528$ environments, with the interval on the paired difference excluding zero. A
bounded correction needs enough authority to matter, and below that threshold it
contributes noise rather than adaptation; no value of $\alpha$ should therefore
be read as incidental.
Whether the adaptation path contributes anything to closed-loop task
performance is a separate question, settled by the paired comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:eval:adaptation} and not asserted here.

\section{IsaacLab Integration and Timing}
\label{sec:ctrl:integration}

Four rates run concurrently and Table~\ref{tab:sys:rates} lists them: PhysX
integrates at $\unit[120]{Hz}$, the policy acts at $\unit[60]{Hz}$ under a
decimation of two, the learned actuator models advance on a $\unit[20]{Hz}$
grid of $\unit[0.05]{s}$ model ticks, and the AAM updates on that same grid. No
single one of these is ``the control frequency'', and reporting a scalar rate
for this system would misdescribe every one of the three stacks.

The relationship between the $\unit[60]{Hz}$ and $\unit[20]{Hz}$ layers is where
integration errors hide, so it is fixed by construction rather than by
interpolation. A learned actuator model is evaluated on every third control step
and its predicted increment is held across the two intervening steps. The action
presented to the model is the action of the tick on which it fires, not an
average, and the target integrator accumulates in reference space, because
integrating against the measured extension turns a static hold into a slow
ratchet (Chapter~\ref{chap:system}).

Order within a tick is fixed and asserted at run time. The history encoder
consumes the window ending at the previous tick and produces its estimate before
the actuator path runs, so no quantity computed at the current tick can
influence its own estimate. History buffers are per-environment and cleared on
episode boundaries, since the vectorised environments reset asynchronously and a
shared buffer would leak one episode into the next. Both properties are checked
rather than assumed: a runtime check replays a trace and asserts bit-exact
agreement between the online estimate and an offline causal recomputation.
Checkpoint loading then compares the stored normalisation statistics, feature
contract and history length of every learned component against what the
environment expects, and aborts on a mismatch.

\section{Constraints and Fair-Comparison Rules}
\label{sec:ctrl:constraints}

\subsection{Limits and Safeguards}

Authority is bounded at four independent places, and the redundancy is
deliberate because each bound protects against a different failure. The action
chain of Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:ppo} limits the rate of change of the command,
fades authority as cylinder travel headroom is consumed, and caps how far the
integrated target may lead the measured extension. The actuator path clips the
valve command to its per-axis limits. The environment terminates an episode on
the safety conditions of Section~\ref{sec:sys:evaluation-criteria} rather than
letting it accumulate reward in a state an operator would stop. And every
learned component falls back to a defined behaviour on a non-finite output.

Preflight runs before any training or evaluation and refuses to start on a
violated precondition. The check that does the most work asserts that the
commanded reference stays inside the measured actuator authority ceiling of
Section~\ref{sec:sys:capability}: the cylinders move the cutter laterally at
most $\unit[0.240]{m/s}$, so a path family demanding more is infeasible by
construction and no reward, critic or optimiser change could recover it.
Terrain is derived from that ceiling, and the preflight assertion keeps the two
from drifting apart.

\subsection{Matching Rules and Comparison Controls}
\label{sec:ctrl:matching}

This thesis does not present a three-way ranking of the stacks. Two distinct
comparisons are made, each with its own matching rule and each carrying one
claim; conflating them would produce a table whose rows are not commensurable.

\paragraph{The Matched Pair: R Against IF}
The representation claim rests on \ppoROM{} and \ppoIDFD, which agree on every
recorded training field and differ only in the actuator path. Held identical
across the pair are the verge task and its eleven path families, the terrain
generator and the held-out mesh set, the reward function of
Table~\ref{tab:ctrl:reward}, the action chain and its authority budget, the
eight acceptance gates and their aggregation to the worst mesh and worst path
family, episode length, actor and critic capacity, and the three-layer training
construction of distillation, critic warm-up and reference-anchored PPO. That
pair is a controlled single-variable comparison and is the only place an
across-stack number is read as such.

One departure is declared. Because the command ROM plus PhysX \emph{is} the
deployment plant (Section~\ref{sec:models:rom-calibration}), \ppoROM's tracking
advantage over \ppoIDFD{} is not evidence that a reduced-order model
approximates the actuator better than an identified pair: one of the two defines
the target.

\paragraph{Non-Comparability of AF and IF}
\ppoAAMFD{} does not share the action contract of the other two, since its
policy emits the valve command itself where the other two emit a bounded desired
cylinder increment that a downstream path realises. The quantity the policy
chooses is not the same quantity in the two cases, so the action chain that
makes the other comparison fair does not extend to this one. This is a declared
property of the implementation, and it is why no row of any table ranks
\ppoAAMFD{} against \ppoIDFD{} on task error. The adaptive claim was never a
claim about beating the non-adaptive stack.

\paragraph{The Paired Comparison: AF Against Its Own Frozen Base}
The adaptation claim is made within the adaptive stack, against the frozen base
policy it was built from, and the matching is exact. The arms share the frozen
base policy, the residual architecture, the trainable capacity of $53\,836$
parameters, the training budget and the reward. Because the head of $g_\psi$ is
zero-initialised and $\tanh(0)=0$, every arm begins as bit-for-bit the same
policy, verified at a maximum absolute action difference of $0.0$. The single
manipulated variable is whether the residual may see the
$(\text{state},\, \text{action})$ history it is conditioned on. A
capacity-matched control is trained at every seed, so the comparison is made
within a seed rather than across one at all five
(Section~\ref{sec:eval:attribution}).

The arms are therefore paired environment by environment, so scenario variation
is differenced out rather than averaged over, and any separation between the
control and the adaptive arm is attributable to online sensing rather than to
added parameters, a different optimiser trajectory or a different checkpoint.
Every arm is scored at the peak of its own training-reward curve by one parser,
which uses no held-out information. The result is reported on this basis in
Section~\ref{sec:eval:adaptation}.

\paragraph{Fine-Tuning Budgets Must Be Matched or Reported}
Comparing a distilled baseline against a separately optimised adaptive policy
and attributing the difference to adaptation is not permitted, for the reason
established in Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:finding}: reward ascent moves a policy off
the acceptance criterion, so an unmatched budget is itself a treatment. The
paired construction above satisfies this rule by design. The rule is stated
because it constrains any future stack added to this comparison, not because the
present one is at risk of violating it.
