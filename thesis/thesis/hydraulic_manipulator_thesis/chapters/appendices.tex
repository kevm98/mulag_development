\section{Closed-Loop Curriculum Generation}
\label{app:data:curriculum}

The procedure that produced the $1\,000$-run Simscape curriculum of
Section~\ref{sec:data:randomization}. No reported model is trained on it; it is
recorded so the collection can be reproduced and audited.

\begin{algorithm}[tb]
  \small
  \DontPrintSemicolon
  \SetKwInOut{Input}{input}\SetKwInOut{Output}{output}
  \Input{run budget $N=1000$; joint limits $\bm{q}_{\min},\bm{q}_{\max}$;
    nominal parameters $\bm{\theta}_0$}
  \Output{dataset $\mathcal{D}=\{(\text{trajectory}_i,\bm{\xi}_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$}
  \BlankLine
  $\mathbf{L}\leftarrow\textsc{LatinHypercube}(N,12)$
    \tcp*{one design for the whole batch}
  \For{$i\leftarrow 1$ \KwTo $N$}{
    assign $i$ to a curriculum block (nominal / hydraulic / mechanical /
      full)\;
    $\bm{\xi}_i\leftarrow\textsc{DrawParameters}(\mathbf{L}_i,\text{block})$
      \tcp*{$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{air}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{vol}},c_d,
      w_{\mathrm{seal}},p_{\mathrm{set}}$; masses, CoM, inertia,
      stiffness}
  }
  \For{$i\leftarrow 1$ \KwTo $N$}{
    inject $\bm{\xi}_i$ into the Simscape model\;
    $n_w\leftarrow\textsc{RandInt}(12,20)$;\quad
      $\bm{W}\leftarrow\textsc{LatinHypercube}(n_w,\text{JDOF})$ within
      $[\bm{q}_{\min},\bm{q}_{\max}]$\;
    assign each segment a speed class (slow / medium / fast / variable);
      \textbf{no} dwell\;
    $\bm{q}_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)\leftarrow\textsc{Interpolate}(\bm{W})$ over
      $\unit[22]{s}$\;
    simulate with \texttt{ode23t}; log on the $\unit[0.05]{s}$ grid\;
    store $(\bm{u},\bm{x},\dot{\bm{x}},\Delta\bm{p},\bm{F},\bm{q},
      \dot{\bm{q}},\bm{q}_{\mathrm{ref}},\bm{\xi}_i)$\;
    \lIf{$i \bmod 100 = 0$}{checkpoint $\mathcal{D}$ to disk}
  }
  \caption{Closed-loop curriculum generation.}
  \label{alg:data:closed-loop}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Per-Environment Spread and the Choice of Statistic}
\label{app:paired-spread}

Every closed-loop comparison in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} is reported as a
paired median difference rather than as a mean or a ratio. Figure~\ref{fig:app:paired}
shows why, by drawing every environment's paired difference instead of
summarising it.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/appendix/app_paired_differences}
  \caption{Per-environment paired differences, sorted, pooled over the three
  seeds on held-out mesh 3141. Positive is better. The dashed curve in the left
  column is the A/A control of Section~\ref{sec:eval:adaptation}, in which both
  arms are the same frozen policy and every difference is therefore noise.}
  \label{fig:app:paired}
\end{figure}

Two properties of the spread govern how any of these comparisons may be read.

The distribution is wide and two-lobed. At $4.0\times$ the paired differences
run from roughly $-1100$ to $+1050\,$mm on cut height while the median
difference is $+71.5\,$mm, so the centre of the distribution and its extremes
carry different information and no single environment is evidence of anything.

More importantly, the A/A control has almost the same spread. Two runs of the
identical frozen policy, differing only in the fifth significant figure of the
payload, disagree by up to $\pm 1000\,$mm on individual environments while their
median difference is $-0.65\,$mm. The per-environment scatter is therefore
dominated by chaotic divergence of the contact dynamics rather than by anything
the treatment does, and it is not reduced by running longer or by adding
environments.

What separates the two curves is the offset, not the shape: the treatment curve
sits above the A/A curve across the whole range, which is the effect the paired
median and its bootstrap interval measure. A mean would be governed by the tails
that the A/A control shows to be noise, and a median log-ratio changes sign
against the marginal medians on a two-lobed distribution
(Section~\ref{sec:eval:instrument}). The paired median difference and the
fraction of environments improved are the two summaries that survive this
spread, and they are what the chapter reports.

\section{Reference-Anchored PPO in Full}
\label{app:anchored-ppo}

The five-stage procedure summarised in Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:anchored}. The
teacher is used only in Stage~1; every PPO rollout is controlled by
$\pi_\vartheta$ alone, and the frozen reference advances only through
checkpoints that pass every gate.

\begin{algorithm}[!ht]
  \small
  \DontPrintSemicolon
  \SetKwInOut{Input}{input}\SetKwInOut{Output}{output}
  \Input{teacher $\pi_T$; gate test
    $\bm{G}(\vartheta)\preceq\bm{g}^{\star}$; $M$ PPO blocks; anchored loss
    and divergence bounds from Equations~\eqref{eq:ctrl:anchored-loss}
    and~\eqref{eq:ctrl:bounds}}
  \Output{last promoted actor $\vartheta^{\star}$ satisfying the gate test}
  \tcp{\textbf{Stage 1 -- Distil a feasible actor}}
  $\mathcal{D}\leftarrow$ DAgger states labelled by teacher mean action
    $\bm{y}^{T}$\;
  $\vartheta \leftarrow \arg\min_{\vartheta}
    \mathbb{E}_{(\bm{o},\bm{y}^{T})\sim\mathcal{D}}
    \bigl[\|\mu_{\vartheta}(\bm{o})-\bm{y}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}\bigr]$\;
  remove $\pi_T$ from the controller
    \tcp*{all later rollouts are teacher-free}
  \tcp{\textbf{Stage 2 -- Warm the critic and set the first anchor}}
  freeze $\vartheta$ and the observation normalisers\;
  \For{$j\leftarrow1$ \KwTo $150$}{
    roll out $\pi_{\vartheta}$ and set
    $\psi \leftarrow \psi - \eta_{\mathrm{critic}}
      \nabla_{\psi}\mathcal{L}_{V}(\psi)$\;
  }
  verify $\vartheta_{\mathrm{after}}=\vartheta_{\mathrm{before}}$\;
  $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}\leftarrow\vartheta$;
    $\vartheta^{\star}\leftarrow\vartheta$
    \tcp*{initial verified anchor}
  \For{block $m\leftarrow0$ \KwTo $M-1$}{
    \tcp{\textbf{Stage 3 -- Train inside the frozen reference bound}}
    freeze $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ for the whole block\;
    \For{each PPO iteration in block $m$}{
      collect an on-policy rollout with $\pi_{\vartheta}$ as sole controller\;
      compute and normalise GAE; save snapshot $\mathcal{S}$;
        set $\vartheta_{\mathrm{old}}\leftarrow\vartheta$\;
      \For{$3$ epochs over the rollout minibatches}{
        \lIf{$\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\mathrm{old}}\|\pi_{\vartheta})
          >\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}$}{stop the update epochs}
        update actor and critic using Equation~\eqref{eq:ctrl:anchored-loss}\;
      }
      compute $(D_{\mathrm{upd}},D_{\mathrm{ref}})$ on the full rollout
        \tcp*{full rollout}
      \uIf{$D_{\mathrm{upd}}$ or $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is non-finite}{
        restore the complete snapshot $\mathcal{S}$\;
      }
      \uElseIf{$D_{\mathrm{upd}}>\delta_{\mathrm{upd}}$ \textbf{or}
        $D_{\mathrm{ref}}>\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}$}{
        restore the actor and its optimiser moments from $\mathcal{S}$;
          retain the new critic fit\;
      }
    }
    \tcp{\textbf{Stage 4 -- Evaluate the block and promote a passing checkpoint}}
    score every saved checkpoint on at least three held-out terrain meshes\;
    aggregate each gate to the worst mesh and, for tracking gates, the worst
      path family\;
    $\mathcal{C}\leftarrow
      \{\vartheta_c:\bm{G}(\vartheta_c)\preceq\bm{g}^{\star}\}$\;
    \eIf{$\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing$}{
      choose $\vartheta^{\star}\in\mathcal{C}$ using the fixed checkpoint
        selection rule\;
      load $\vartheta\leftarrow\vartheta^{\star}$ and set
        $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}\leftarrow\vartheta^{\star}$\;
      reset the actor optimiser moments; retain the critic state\;
    }{
      leave $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ unchanged; do not promote the block\;
    }
  }
  \tcp{\textbf{Stage 5 -- Return the deployable policy}}
  \Return{$\vartheta^{\star}=\vartheta_{\mathrm{ref}}$}\;
  \caption{Five-stage reference-anchored PPO procedure. The teacher is used
  only in Stage~1. All PPO rollouts are controlled by $\pi_{\vartheta}$ alone,
  and the frozen reference moves only when a checkpoint passes every gate.}
  \label{alg:ctrl:anchored}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Derivation of the Reference-Divergence Gate Bound}
\label{app:kl-bound}

Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:theory} states, without showing the steps, that a bound
on the per-step reference divergence $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ implies a bound on
gate degradation of the form
$|\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\vartheta}[g] - \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}[g]|
\le 2 G_{\max}\sqrt{H \delta_{\mathrm{ref}}/2}$. This appendix derives that
bound and recomputes it at the production horizon, since the number quoted in
the main text does not match evaluating the formula it cites.

\paragraph{Setup} Let $P_\pi$ denote the distribution over length-$H$
trajectories $\tau = (s_0, a_0, \ldots, s_{H-1}, a_{H-1})$ induced by rolling
out policy $\pi$ under the fixed environment transition kernel. Because the
transition kernel is shared by $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $\pi_\vartheta$, it
cancels from the log-ratio between the two trajectory distributions, and the
chain rule of KL divergence reduces to a sum of per-step, per-state action
divergences:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{KL}(P_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} \,\|\, P_{\pi_\vartheta})
  = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim P_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}}
    \left[\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}
    \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid s_t)\,\|\,
    \pi_\vartheta(\cdot\mid s_t)\bigr)\right].
  \label{eq:app:kl-chain}
\end{equation}
This step is exact only if the per-step divergences on the right are evaluated
on states drawn from $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$'s own rollout distribution. In the
actual training loop $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is evaluated on states visited by the
\emph{live} policy $\pi_\vartheta$ (Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:anchored}), not by
$\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, so what follows is the idealised argument the main text
already flags as carrying a measure mismatch, not a derivation that closes
that gap.

\paragraph{Chain rule bound} The chain rule for KL divergence is
standard~\cite{cover2006information}. Bounding every one of the $H$ per-step
terms by
the training-time average bound $\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ctrl:bounds} gives
$\mathrm{KL}(P_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} \| P_{\pi_\vartheta}) \le H\,\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}$.

\paragraph{Pinsker's inequality} \cite{cover2006information} For any two
distributions $P, Q$ on a common space, total variation distance
$\delta_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q) := \sup_A |P(A) - Q(A)|$ satisfies
$\delta_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q) \le \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{KL}(P\|Q)}$. Applied to
the trajectory distributions,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}, P_{\pi_\vartheta})
  \le \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2} H \delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} .
  \label{eq:app:tv-bound}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Bounded-functional bound} For a trajectory functional
$g:\mathcal{T}\to[0, G_{\max}]$, such as any of the eight gate metrics, the
standard identity $\sum_\tau |P(\tau)-Q(\tau)| = 2\,\delta_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)$
gives
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\mathbb{E}_{P_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}}[g] - \mathbb{E}_{P_{\pi_\vartheta}}[g]\bigr|
  \le G_{\max} \sum_\tau |P_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(\tau) - P_{\pi_\vartheta}(\tau)|
  = 2\, G_{\max}\, \delta_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}, P_{\pi_\vartheta}) .
  \label{eq:app:functional-bound}
\end{equation}
Combining Equations~\eqref{eq:app:tv-bound} and~\eqref{eq:app:functional-bound}
gives the quoted bound,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\vartheta}[g] - \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}[g]\bigr|
  \le 2\, G_{\max} \sqrt{\tfrac{H\,\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}}{2}} .
  \label{eq:app:final-bound}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Numeric value at the production horizon} At $H = 2808$ steps and
$\delta_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.05$, $\sqrt{H\delta_{\mathrm{ref}}/2} =
\sqrt{70.2} \approx 8.38$. For a gate metric normalised to $[0,1]$,
$G_{\max}=1$, so Equation~\eqref{eq:app:final-bound} evaluates to
$2 \times 8.38 \approx 16.76$, the value quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:ctrl:theory}. A bound of $16.76$ on a $[0,1]$-valued metric is
vacuous, and it does not close the measure mismatch noted above. The
construction remains a principled restriction of reward ascent to a
neighbourhood of a verified policy, not a certificate.

\section{Reproduction Entry Points}
\label{app:repro}

The closed-loop results of Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} are generated by the
scripts under \texttt{experiments/E1} through \texttt{experiments/E6}. Each
experiment directory records the evaluated model, mesh set, trajectory families,
carrier speed and scored horizon in its manifest. AF arms are trained and scored
under \texttt{PPO\_AAM\_FD/experiment\_scripts}. The adaptation tables and figures
are recomputed from \texttt{outputs/final}, \texttt{outputs/heldout8191},
\texttt{outputs/aa\_floor} and \texttt{outputs/kappa\_loaded} by
\texttt{final\_table.py}, \texttt{robust\_table.py},
\texttt{heldout8191\_table.py}, \texttt{wronginfo\_table.py},
\texttt{make\_adaptation\_figure.py}, \texttt{make\_kappa\_figure.py} and
\texttt{make\_appendix\_figure.py} in that directory, so every reported number
and every plotted point comes from the same stored reports. The paired cross-stack statistics
of Table~\ref{tab:eval:paired-rif}, including the leave-one-family-out
aggregate, are recomputed from the frozen E1 reports by
\texttt{experiments/E1/code/rif\_paired.py}. Model-level results use the frozen
collections described in Appendix~\ref{app:data:frozen}.

Each pre-registered comparison has a dated design note written before its arms
were trained, under \texttt{reports/}: the success rule, the checkpoint rule,
the statistic and the declared outcomes are fixed there and are not re-decided
after the seeds are seen. Together with the artifact digests in
Table~\ref{tab:app:hashes}, these records identify the data, models, rules and
evaluation code behind the reported results.
