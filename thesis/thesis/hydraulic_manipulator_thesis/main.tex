\RRLABabstract{Abstract}{%
Hydraulic manipulators are difficult to control precisely: the actuator response
is nonlinear, depends on load and supply pressure, and varies between machines.
Faithful simulation is possible but far too slow for reinforcement learning,
while commanding joint positions directly discards the behaviour that makes the
machine difficult.

This thesis investigates how hydraulic actuator dynamics learned from
high-fidelity simulation can be reduced, transferred into an efficient robotic
simulation, and used for reinforcement-learning control of a verge mowing arm.
Two actuator representations are identified from Simscape campaigns: a modular
chain of an inverse model, mapping desired cylinder motion to a valve command,
and a forward model predicting the response; and a physics-structured
reduced-order model keeping pressure, force and extension propagation explicit
while learning covers valve realisation and loading. Both serve as the actuation
interface for policy learning. A damped least-squares controller supplies
privileged demonstrations and is then removed, so the distilled policy becomes
the sole deployed controller.

The inverse and forward pair transfers to the deployment simulation without
retraining in the one-step regime the policy uses, where extension error stays
millimetre-scale across plant domains, and the reduced-order model transfers
with a small bounded calibration against the deployment plant. Supervised
accuracy proves an unreliable guide to control: a forward model scored highly
while learning to ignore the valve command, and excitation design with input
ablation are established as the tests that expose it. Both representations
support closed-loop terrain following, and the choice of representation changes
the control strategy the policy learns.

Online adaptation is examined last. Plant identity proves recoverable only from
deliberate excitation, and under the randomised plant family the trained stacks
absorb the variation unaided, so adaptation is tested on a plant whose load
changes mid-episode. There a bounded correction on the inverse model's output
changes nothing, and the reason is delivery rather than estimation: that command
already rests against its limit for most of the scored window, so the authority
to act is absent where the information arrives. Removing the inverse model, so
that the policy emits the valve command and a bounded residual conditioned on
causal history acts upon it, converts the same information into a substantial
gain. A capacity-matched control with its history window zeroed does not deliver
that gain, so it belongs to the live history rather than to the added
parameters, and sweeping the magnitude of the plant change leaves a lateral
component that grows with it, which is online adaptation in the strict sense.
The gain is large on the seeds where the method works and does not hold on the
median of five. The thesis contributes a decision procedure for whether online
adaptation is worth building on a machine, and the finding that on a saturating
actuator the placement of the correction, not the quality of the estimate,
decides whether adaptation pays.
}
