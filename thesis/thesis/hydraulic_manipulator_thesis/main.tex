\RRLABabstract{Abstract}{%
Hydraulic manipulators are difficult to control precisely: the actuator response
is nonlinear, depends on load and supply pressure, and varies between machines.
Faithful simulation is possible but far too slow for reinforcement learning,
while commanding joint positions directly discards the behaviour that makes the
machine difficult.

This thesis investigates how hydraulic actuator dynamics learned from
high-fidelity simulation can be reduced, transferred into an efficient robotic
simulation, and used for reinforcement-learning control of a verge mowing arm.
Actuator data from a high-fidelity Simscape model are used to identify a modular
inverse and forward model chain and a physics-structured reduced-order model.
The frozen inverse and forward representation transfers from Simscape without
deployment-specific retraining, whereas the reduced-order model receives a
bounded calibration of its correction head against the deployment plant. The
former maps desired cylinder motion to valve commands and predicts the resulting
response; the latter keeps pressure, force and extension propagation explicit
while learning valve realisation and unmodelled loading. A damped
least-squares controller supplies privileged demonstrations and is then removed,
leaving the distilled policy as the sole deployed controller.

Both actuator representations are realised in the deployment simulation and
support closed-loop terrain following. The evaluation also shows why supervised
accuracy alone is insufficient: a model can predict motion well while ignoring
the valve command that a controller must use. Excitation design and input
ablation are therefore treated as necessary checks before a learned actuator
model is admitted to control.

The transferred models and trained policies already absorb much of the tested
plant variation. A bounded residual is therefore added on the valve-command
path, where correction authority remains. Under the pre-registered checkpoint
rule and success criterion the residual improves tracking on four of five
training seeds; three of those seeds improve both channels, and the median seed
still passes at the fixed final checkpoint. A capacity-matched residual reading
zeroed history reproduces most of that gain, so added residual capacity rather
than live history accounts for the median improvement. Live history separates
from the zero-history control on one seed, and a cutter-mass dose study resolves
an additional lateral component that grows with plant load. An earlier
latent-conditioned correction on a saturated inverse command has no closed-loop
effect. Online adaptation is therefore useful here as a bounded residual with
remaining authority; attributing the median gain to plant identification
requires the history-versus-capacity split, which is resolved only on particular
seeds.
}
