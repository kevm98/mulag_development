\RRLABabstract{Abstract}{%
Hydraulic manipulators are difficult to control precisely: the actuator response
is nonlinear, depends on load and supply pressure, and varies between machines.
Faithful simulation of those effects is possible but far too slow for
reinforcement learning, while commanding joint positions directly discards the
behaviour that makes the machine difficult.

This thesis investigates how hydraulic actuator dynamics learned from a
high-fidelity Simscape model can be reduced, transferred into an efficient
robotic simulation and used for reinforcement-learning control of a verge mowing
arm. Two actuator representations are identified from the same data: a modular
inverse and forward chain, and a physics-structured reduced-order model that
keeps pressure, force, velocity and extension propagation explicit while
learning valve realisation and unmodelled loading. The inverse and forward pair
transfers from Simscape frozen, without deployment-specific retraining; the
reduced-order model receives a bounded calibration of its correction head
against the deployment plant. A model can also score well on supervised
prediction while ignoring the valve command a controller must use, so excitation
design and command ablation are established as admission tests for a learned
actuator.

Both representations are integrated into the deployment simulation and used for
policy learning. A damped least-squares controller supplies privileged
demonstrations during training and is then removed, leaving the distilled policy
as the sole deployed controller, emitting desired cylinder motion into learned
actuator dynamics. Both stacks support closed-loop terrain following, and a
matched comparison over held-out meshes, trajectory families and reserved plant
configurations shows that the choice of representation changes tracking, command
behaviour, dynamic response and robustness under plant variation. The
transferred models and trained policies already absorb much of the tested
variation unaided.

Online adaptation is examined last, as two mechanisms rather than one. The
explicit mechanism estimates a plant latent from recent interaction history and
corrects the inverse command with it. It is informative but undeliverable:
latent-conditioned adapters beat a zero-context control on the adapter objective
on all five seeds, yet no closed-loop metric changes, because the inverse command
is saturated for most of the scored window and the correction is clipped before
it reaches the plant. The implicit mechanism conditions a bounded residual on a
causal history window and applies it to the valve command, where authority
remains. It improves tracking on four of five training seeds under the
pre-registered rule, while a capacity-matched residual reading zeroed history
reproduces most of that gain. Effective control of this machine therefore
depends not only on model accuracy and policy robustness, but on where the
correction acts and on whether live history adds information beyond residual
capacity.
}
