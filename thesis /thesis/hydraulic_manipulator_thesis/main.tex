\RRLABabstract{Abstract}{%
TODO: abstract. Hydraulic manipulators are hard to control precisely because
the actuator response is nonlinear, load dependent, and uncertain. This thesis
asks how the learned representation of that actuator affects closed-loop task
performance, and whether conditioning that representation online on an
inferred estimate of the currently active plant improves on a fixed learned
model. Three control stacks are compared under matched task, observation,
action, terrain, reward, seed and budget conditions, differing only in the
actuation and adaptation path: \ppoROM, in which the policy's desired cylinder
motion is realized by a command-conditioned hydraulic reduced-order model;
\ppoIDFD, in which it is realized by a frozen inverse model followed by a
frozen strict forward model; and \ppoIDAAMFD, in which an Arm Adaptation
Module infers a dynamics latent from causal observable history and conditions
a small bounded adapter around the frozen inverse model.
}
