Can a hydraulic arm
learn to adapt
while it works?

A simulated MULAG verge mower must keep its cutter on a changing path while terrain and payload disturb the arm. This project studies what learned actuator models and online payload estimates actually contribute to control.

01The question

Tracking is the objective.
Terrain is the disturbance.

The truck moves along the road. Its hydraulic arm reaches over the embankment, keeping the cutter near a target line while the ground changes below it.

The research asks two linked questions: how much does the actuator representation matter to closed-loop control, and when can an online estimate of changing payload help a frozen policy?

02Research principles

A result is only as strong as its comparison.

01 /

Pair the conditions

Compare policies on the same mesh and trajectory family. Report paired differences and the number of conditions that improve, rather than relying on marginal medians.

02 /

Verify the plant

Check that a payload change actually reached the simulated plant before interpreting the controller's response.

03 /

Validate the measurement

Measure instrument variation and state the checkpoint and scoring horizon. A small apparent gain can sit below the measurement floor.

04 /

Keep claims bounded

Separate model prediction from control performance, and simulation findings from physical deployment.

04What the experiments show

The interesting result is conditional.

01 / Online adaptation

Heavy payload creates room for adaptation to help.

On held-out mesh 3141, pooled paired medians across three context-training seeds improve cut-height error, lateral error, and ground contact at 4× and 5× payload. The base policy is frozen; only context input weights are trained.

4× payload+71.5 mmcut-height error improvement
5× payload+82.2 mmcut-height error improvement
4× / 5×+0.12 / +0.09ground-contact fraction

These are pooled paired median differences, not hardware gains. Two of three seeds drive the heavy-load benefit; seed 23 worsens cut height at 5×.

02 / The limit

At 3× payload, the trade turns against lateral tracking.

The frozen controller already maintains contact. Pooled lateral error becomes 22.75 mm worse with adaptation, with a 95% paired interval of 17.27 to 30.04 mm worse. The result points toward activating adaptation only when a measurable deficit exists.

Read the limitation ↗
03 / Actuator comparison

Changing the actuator path did not settle the control question.

For the matched closed-loop comparison, no R versus IF contrast reached significance across 33 mesh–trajectory cells; every paired bootstrap interval crossed zero.

Closed-loop protocol ↗
04 / Plant characterisation

Hydraulic timing matters.

Command-to-differential-pressure onset was measured at 117 ms, or seven 60 Hz control steps, across three of four amplitude tiers. The study distinguishes this measurable transport latency from an actuator model that remains difficult to identify.
